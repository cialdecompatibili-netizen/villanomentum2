Mentana is a village in the **Roman Sabina**, about 20 km from Rome, with a history tied to the 1867 battle and to an even older past. It can be visited in half a day and combines well with a trip to Rome.

## The Garibaldi Museum (MuGa)

The museum commemorates the **Battle of Mentana of 3 November 1867**, fought by Garibaldi's volunteers for the liberation of Rome. The building, erected in 1905, looks like a temple and bears the inscription "Roma o morte" on its facade. Inside are relics of the 1867 campaign and, in the renovated route, a multimedia room that recreates the battle with sound and light. It is on via della Rocca.

## The Parco della Rimembranza and the ossuary altar

Next to the museum is a park of about 3,000 square metres with centuries-old pines, dedicated to the fallen Garibaldians. Inside is the ossuary altar inaugurated in 1887. It is a quiet place, good for a walk even if you are not a history enthusiast.

## The archaeological museum (MuCaM)

In the Galleria Borghese, in the historic centre, the museum collects finds from ancient **Nomentum**, the Roman municipium that occupied this area: an altar dedicated to Vulcan, sarcophagi and architectural fragments. The visit ends with a walk through the village lanes, where ancient marble pieces can be seen.

## Nature: the Marcigliana reserve

Just outside Mentana, between the Tiber valley and the Monti Cornicolani, is the **Marcigliana Nature Reserve**, covering about a thousand hectares of countryside. It is good for walks and for anyone who loves greenery without going far from Rome. If you prefer cycling, in northern Rome there is the **Tiber cycle path**, a route of about 35 km along the river.

## What to eat

The Sabina is known for its **Sabina DOP extra virgin olive oil**. In Mentana there are festivals dedicated to specialities such as the *ciammella a cancellu* and the *maccheroni a centonara*, as well as the traditional celebrations of Saint Anthony Abbot.
