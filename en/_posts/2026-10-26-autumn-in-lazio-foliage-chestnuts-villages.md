Autumn in Lazio means day trips under two hours from Rome: woods changing colour, villages, food festivals and hearty dishes. The best period for foliage runs from mid-October to mid-November.

## Foliage

- **Monte Livata and the Simbruini Mountains:** the beech woods turn yellow, orange and red in October and November. Monte Livata sits at about 1,350 metres, in the Subiaco area. The Simbruini are the largest protected area in Lazio, with a dense trail network.
- **Monte Cimino beech forest:** near Soriano nel Cimino in Tuscia, a UNESCO site, above 1,000 metres.
- **San Liberato Park:** near Lake Bracciano, with Canadian maples and Japanese cherry trees.
- **Lepini Mountains:** karst caves and peaks such as Monte Semprevisa.

## Lakes and villages

- **Lake Bolsena:** golden shores and lake cuisine, such as fish soup.
- **Lake Bracciano and Trevignano:** lakeside walks and views from the Rocca.
- **Lake Vico and Ronciglione:** chestnut and beech woods, mushrooms and chestnuts.
- **Civita di Bagnoregio, Sermoneta, Subiaco and Calcata:** villages at their best in autumn.

## Food and festivals

The **Castelli Romani** mean grape harvest, chestnuts and hearty dishes (Ariccia is known for porchetta). In Tuscia it is new olive oil season, with legume soups and chestnut dishes. Food festivals for mushrooms, chestnuts, truffles and pumpkin change every year: check dates and programmes on the town websites.

## Practical tips

- **Foliage is short-lived:** a week can change everything, so don't wait.
- **Leave in the morning:** traffic is calmer.
- **Hiking shoes and layers** for the higher-altitude spots.
