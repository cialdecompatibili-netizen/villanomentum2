Staying in Mentana and spending the day in Rome works well, as long as you plan ahead. This is a classic itinerary, designed to see the essentials without rushing.

## How to get there

Take the Cotral bus or the FL1 train to **Roma Tiburtina**, then **metro line B** to the **Colosseo** stop. Details and times are in the guide on [how to get to Rome from Mentana]({% include url-ref.html ref="come-arrivare" %}).

## Morning: Colosseum and Roman Forum

Leave early, perhaps after breakfast in the garden, so you reach the Colosseum with a shorter queue. The ticket usually also covers the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill area: if you want to avoid queues, consider buying it online in advance and check the current entry rules.

## Lunch: the Monti district

A few steps from the Colosseum is the Monti district, full of trattorias and restaurants. For a quick lunch a slice of pizza or a sandwich is enough, so you do not lose time.

## Afternoon: Pantheon, Piazza Navona and Trevi Fountain

From here you go on foot, through lanes and squares: the **Pantheon**, **Piazza Navona** and the **Trevi Fountain**. They are all close to each other, so they make a good walk of a few hours. The Trevi Fountain is crowded at every hour: go whenever you prefer, but do not expect to have it to yourself.

## Evening: the return

Before leaving, check the **last service** of the bus or train back to Mentana, especially if you stay for dinner. If you want to have dinner at a relaxed pace, leave the centre with the right margin.

## Tips

- **Comfortable shoes**: you walk a lot on cobblestones.
- **Water**: Rome has many free drinking fountains.
- **Opening times**: check them beforehand, they change during the year.
- **More days?** With three or four days you can add the Vatican Museums, Trastevere and Villa Borghese.

If you want to sleep in a quiet area and get around Rome by bus and train, see the page for [visitors]({% include url-ref.html ref="turisti" %}) and [ask for availability]({% include url-ref.html ref="contatti" %}).
